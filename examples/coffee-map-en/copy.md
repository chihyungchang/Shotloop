# Where does the world's coffee come from

## Idea

Where the world's coffee comes from: the band of tropical countries that grow it, the few that dominate, and the routes that carry it to the cups of Europe and North America.

## Hook

About 170 million bags of coffee are grown every year. Where?

## Copy

Around 170 million 60-kilo bags of coffee are produced every year.

Almost all of it grows in a band around the equator, between the tropics: the coffee belt, more than 70 countries.

One country leads by far: Brazil grows about a third of the world's coffee.

Next come Vietnam, mostly robusta, then Colombia, Indonesia and Ethiopia.

Ethiopia is where arabica coffee began.

From the belt, the beans sail north: the European Union and the United States are the biggest importers.

Every cup, a long journey.

## Facts

- World coffee production is about 170 million 60-kg bags a year. (source: International Coffee Organization, Coffee Market Report (2023/24 crop year), https://www.ico.org)
- Coffee is grown in more than 70 countries, almost all between the Tropic of Cancer and the Tropic of Capricorn (the 'coffee belt'). (source: National Coffee Association USA, 'Where Coffee Grows', https://www.ncausa.org)
- Brazil is the largest producer, with about a third of world output (≈66 of ≈175 million bags ≈ 38%). (source: USDA Foreign Agricultural Service, Coffee: World Markets and Trade (2024), https://www.fas.usda.gov)
- The top five producers are Brazil, Vietnam, Colombia, Indonesia and Ethiopia; Vietnam grows mostly robusta. (source: USDA Foreign Agricultural Service, Coffee: World Markets and Trade (2024), https://www.fas.usda.gov)
- Arabica coffee originated in the highlands of Ethiopia. (source: Encyclopaedia Britannica, 'Coffee', https://www.britannica.com/topic/coffee)
- The European Union and the United States are the largest coffee importers. (source: USDA Foreign Agricultural Service, Coffee: World Markets and Trade (2024), https://www.fas.usda.gov)
