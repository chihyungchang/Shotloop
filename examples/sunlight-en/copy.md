# How long does sunlight take to reach Earth?

## Idea

You never see the Sun as it is now. Its light needs time to cross the
distance to Earth: 1 astronomical unit divided by the speed of light is
about 499 s, 8 min 19 s. So the sunlight on your face left the Sun about
8 minutes ago.

## Facts

- Mean Sun–Earth distance: 1 au = 149,597,870,700 m exactly ≈ 149.6 million km
  (IAU 2012 Resolution B2).
- Speed of light in vacuum: c = 299,792,458 m/s exactly ≈ 299,792 km/s
  (SI definition of the metre, BIPM).
- Light time for 1 au: 149,597,870.7 km ÷ 299,792.458 km/s = 499.0 s
  = 8 min 19 s (NASA "Sun" facts give "about 8 minutes").
- Earth's equator: 40,075 km, so c ≈ 7.5 equatorial laps per second.
- Moon: mean distance 384,400 km ÷ c ≈ 1.28 s ≈ 1.3 s (NASA Moon facts).
- Neptune: about 30.07 au from the Sun (NASA Neptune facts) → 30.07 ×
  499.0 s ≈ 15,005 s ≈ 4.17 h ≈ 4 h 10 min.
- Distances vary along the orbits (Earth 0.983–1.017 au), so these are
  mean values.

- BIPM, The International System of Units (SI brochure, 9th ed.): c
