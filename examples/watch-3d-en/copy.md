# What is inside a mechanical watch, and how does it keep time

## Idea

A mechanical watch keeps time with no battery: a wound spring stores the energy, gears carry it, and an escapement and balance wheel let it out in tiny, even beats.

## Hook

No battery, no chip. So what keeps a mechanical watch ticking?

## Copy

Open the case and there is no battery: just springs, gears and jewels.

Winding the crown coils a flat steel spring, the mainspring. That tight coil is the watch's whole energy supply.

A train of gears carries that energy towards the hands. Left alone, the spring would unwind in seconds.

The escapement stops that: a pallet fork locks the escape wheel, then releases it one tooth at a time.

The beat comes from the balance wheel, swinging back and forth on a hairspring. In many modern watches it beats 8 times a second: about 691,200 ticks a day.

A coiled spring and a swinging wheel: that is the whole secret.

## Facts

- A mechanical watch is powered by a wound mainspring, not a battery. (source: Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Watch" — https://www.britannica.com/technology/watch)
- A gear train transmits the mainspring's energy to the escapement and the hands. (source: Wikipedia, "Mechanical watch" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_watch)
- The escapement (escape wheel and pallet fork) releases the gear train in small, regular steps and gives the balance wheel impulses. (source: Wikipedia, "Lever escapement" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lever_escapement)
- The balance wheel and its hairspring oscillate at a steady rate and are the watch's timekeeping element. (source: Wikipedia, "Balance wheel" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balance_wheel)
- Many modern watches beat at 28,800 vibrations per hour, i.e. 8 beats per second (4 Hz); 28,800 × 24 ≈ 691,200 beats a day. (source: Wikipedia, "Mechanical watch" (beat rates) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_watch; arithmetic: 28,800 per hour × 24 hours = 691,200)
- Watch bearings are jewels, usually synthetic rubies, which reduce friction. (source: Wikipedia, "Jewel bearing" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewel_bearing)
