# How does a mechanical watch tick?

## Idea

A mechanical watch keeps time with no battery: a wound spring stores the energy, gears carry it, and an escapement and balance wheel let it out in tiny, even beats.

## Hook

No battery, no chip. So what keeps a mechanical watch ticking?

## Copy

Open the case and there is no battery: just springs, gears and jewels.

Winding the crown coils a spring, the mainspring. That tight coil stores the energy that powers the watch.

A train of gears carries that energy on, and turns the beats into seconds, minutes and hours on the hands.

The escapement lets it out in steps: with each swing of the balance it lets the gears advance a fixed amount, and gives the balance a small push.

The beat comes from the balance wheel, swinging back and forth on a hairspring. In many modern watches it beats 8 times a second: about 691,200 ticks a day.

A coiled spring and a swinging wheel: that is the whole secret.

## Facts

- A mechanical watch is powered by a wound mainspring, not a battery. Quoted: "A mainspring, which stores mechanical energy to power the watch." (source: Wikipedia, "Mechanical watch", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_watch)
- A gear train carries the mainspring's force to the balance and counts its swings for the hands. Quoted: "A gear train, called the wheel train, which has the dual function of transmitting the force of the mainspring to the balance wheel and adding up the swings of the balance wheel to get units of seconds, minutes, and hours." (source: Wikipedia, "Mechanical watch", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_watch)
- The escapement keeps the balance swinging and lets the gears advance a fixed step with each swing. Quoted: "An escapement is a mechanical linkage that delivers impulses to the timepiece's balance wheel, keeping it oscillating back and forth, and with each swing of the balance wheel allows the timepiece's gear train to advance a fixed amount." (source: Wikipedia, "Lever escapement", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lever_escapement)
- The balance wheel is the watch's timekeeping element. Quoted: "A balance wheel, or balance, is the timekeeping device used in mechanical watches and small clocks" (source: Wikipedia, "Balance wheel", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balance_wheel)
- Many watches beat 8 times a second, 28,800 an hour. Quoted: "Most watch balance wheels oscillate at 5, 6, 8, or 10 beats per second. This translates into 2.5, 3, 4, and 5 Hz respectively, or 18000, 21,600, 28,800, and 36,000 beats per hour (BPH)." A day is our arithmetic, not a quote: 28,800 × 24 = 691,200, shown as "≈691,200 a day". (source: Wikipedia, "Balance wheel", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balance_wheel)
- Watch bearings are jewels, usually synthetic rubies. Quoted: "Jewels in modern watches are all synthetic sapphire or (usually) ruby" (source: Wikipedia, "Jewel bearing", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewel_bearing)
