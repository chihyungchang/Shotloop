# How does a key open a pin tumbler lock · Storyboard

| # | Time | Shot | Motion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 3.6 s | Hook: the lock: Hero image: the lock cutaway, big and lit square on in a dark room, a warm rim light along the brass plug; the key's tip hovers just left of the keyway, pointing right. Housing shown as a clear ghost so the five pin stacks already read inside. Headline in the top band. | Slow push-in of a few centimetres onto the lock; the key eases a little toward the keyway (anticipation) and stops; hold on the end frame. |
| 2 | 6.6 s | Pins block the gap: X-ray ghost: the steel housing fades to a faint wireframe so the cutaway is clear, seen square on. Five springs press five stacks down: blue driver pins on top, copper key pins below. The driver pins cross the glowing cyan gap between plug and housing. The plug tries to turn a few degrees, jams against the blue pins and springs back. Label 'shear line' pinned to the cyan gap, off the subject to the left. | Springs compress and push the stacks down one after another, left to right, as 'springs push' is spoken; on 'can't turn' the plug rattles twice and stops. Camera slow 20° hero orbit from three-quarter view to square on, held at the end. |
| 3 | 6.2 s | Key lifts the pins: Square-on cutaway. The key slides in from the left, its ridged edge riding under the copper key pins; each stack bobs up and down over the bumps and settles at a different height. Tracking with foreground: the key's bow passes close to camera, out of focus, as the camera rides alongside. | Key travels left to right with ease-in and ease-out; pin heights follow the key profile under each pin (driven by the geometry, not by eye), the springs squeezing and stretching; overshoot as the key seats. Camera tracks right with the key, then holds. |
| 4 | 5.8 s | Splits line up: Push-in on the cyan shear line: all five splits between blue and copper pins now sit exactly on it. Each split flashes as it lands, left to right, drawing a continuous bright line across the plug's top. Brief inset beat: a wrong key leaves one copper pin poking above the line, red, then the right key returns. | Slow push-in on realisation from the whole cutaway to the five splits; the cyan line draws left to right as the splits light; hold at the end. |
| 5 | 5.5 s | The cylinder turns: Camera crane round to a three-quarter view: the brass plug and key turn together, the copper pins turning with the plug while the blue pins stay up in the housing. The cam on the plug's back sweeps the bolt; the bolt slides back out of the strike plate into the lock. | Anticipation: a slight back-turn of the key, then the plug rotates 90° with ease and the cam pushes the bolt back right to left; overlapping settle of the bolt. Camera arcs 40° to see the plug's turn, held. |
| 6 | 2.5 s | One pattern: Crane up and back: the whole lock in a door edge, door swinging open a crack, a warm light spilling through. The key profile and the five pin heights glow as matching outlines. | Crane up and pull back, ease out, hold the final frame 1 s. |
