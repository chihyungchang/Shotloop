# 01 · How long to crack your password?

Step 1 of the explainer workflow: the copy. What the episode says, why,
and where every fact comes from. Nothing reaches the script without a
source here.

## Angle

Everyone has seen "how long to crack your password" tables. The twist
most of them bury: the comforting numbers assume a random password that
has never leaked. Real passwords mostly aren't, and attackers try
leaked ones first. So the episode goes: big safe number → it keeps
shrinking → and it doesn't apply to you anyway → what actually works.

Audience: anyone with an online account. Defensive only: no attack
steps, no tools named.

## Hook

"How long would a hacker need to crack YOUR password?"

## Facts (all from Hive Systems, 2026 Password Table)

| Claim in the video | Source wording |
| --- | --- |
| The rig: 16 × RTX 5090, bcrypt | "2026 hardware: 16 x RTX 5090 \| 2026 password hash: bcrypt set to a work order of 10" |
| 8 random lowercase letters: about two weeks | "drops to about two weeks" (blog FAQ); release: "which took three weeks to crack last year, now falls in two" |
| 8 random characters, all types: 132 years | "fallen from 225 years in 2024 to 164 in 2025 to 132 in 2026" |
| 20–25% faster every year | "passwords cracking roughly 20 to 25% faster every year" |
| The table assumes random, never-breached passwords and no MFA | "These metrics assume passwords are randomly generated." "…a password that hasn't been part of a past breach." "…assumes MFA isn't used or has been bypassed" |
| Reused / leaked / dictionary passwords fall almost instantly | "a common, reused, or dictionary-based password can fall instantly." |
| Attackers try leaked passwords first | "Attackers try all common and breached passwords before bothering to crack new ones." |

Sources:
- Hive Systems, press release, 14 Jul 2026: https://www.hivesystems.com/newsroom-posts/07-14-2026
- Hive Systems, "Are your passwords in the green?" (2026 table write-up): https://www.hivesystems.com/blog/are-your-passwords-in-the-green

Not used, on purpose: per-cell values for 12 and 16 characters. The
table publishes them only as an image, and we don't quote what we
can't check in text.

## Takeaway

1. Long and random, from a password manager.
2. Never reuse one.
3. Turn on 2FA: the scary table assumes you didn't.
