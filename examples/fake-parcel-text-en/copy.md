# How a fake parcel text steals your card

## Idea

A fake 'delivery problem' text sends you to a copycat page that asks for a small fee and takes your card details. The video walks through each step on a phone and shows the signs that give it away.

## Hook

Americans reported $470 million lost to text scams in 2024, and fake package-delivery texts were the most reported text scam that year.

## Copy

A text arrives: your parcel is on hold, pay a small redelivery fee. It comes from an unknown number, it pushes you to hurry, and the link looks odd.

Tap it and a page opens that looks just like a courier's site, but the address in the bar isn't theirs.

It asks for a tiny fee, so you type in your card number, expiry date and security code, then press Pay.

Those details go straight to the scammers, who can use the card themselves.

How to beat it: don't tap. Check with the company using a website or number you know is real, not the one in the text. Forward the text to 7726, then delete it.

## Facts

- “In 2024, people reported $470 million in losses to these scams, more than five times the 2020 number.” (source: FTC Data Spotlight, 'Top text scams of 2024', 14 Apr 2025 — https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/data-visualizations/data-spotlight/2025/04/top-text-scams-2024)
- “Messages about package deliveries, usually from someone pretending to be from the U.S. Postal Service, were the most reported text scam last year.” (last year = 2024) (source: FTC Data Spotlight, 'Top text scams of 2024', 14 Apr 2025 — https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/data-visualizations/data-spotlight/2025/04/top-text-scams-2024)
- Scam texts often claim a problem with a delivery and include a link; you shouldn't click links in unexpected texts. (source: FTC Consumer Advice, 'How To Recognize and Report Spam Text Messages' — https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-recognize-and-report-spam-text-messages)
- “Copy the message and forward it to 7726 (SPAM). This helps your wireless provider spot and block similar messages in the future.” (source: FTC Consumer Advice, 'How To Recognize and Report Spam Text Messages' — https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-recognize-and-report-spam-text-messages)
- “If you think the message might be real, contact the company using a phone number or website you know is real. Not the information in the text message.” (source: FTC Consumer Advice, 'How To Recognize and Report Spam Text Messages' — https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-recognize-and-report-spam-text-messages)
- Illustrative, not sourced: the $1.99 fee, the sender number, the parcel-redeliver.info address, the pay page, the card and the charges are made up for the video; no real courier brand or logo is shown.
