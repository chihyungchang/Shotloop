# English words shaped by Chinese

## Idea

Four everyday English words (ketchup, tycoon, typhoon, kowtow) were shaped by Chinese: three come from it, and it may have shaped the fourth (typhoon). Each one hides a surprising original meaning.

## Hook

You speak a little Chinese already. Four everyday English words were shaped by it.

## Copy

Ketchup: it started as a fish sauce. Merriam-Webster traces it, directly or via Malay 'kecap' ('fish sauce'), to Southern Min (Hokkien) 'kê-chiap': 'salted or pickled fish' + 'juice'. Today's ketchup is usually made from tomatoes.

Tycoon: the Chinese 大君 (Mandarin 'dà jūn'), 'great' + 'lord', reached English through the Japanese 'taikun', a title used for the shogun.

Typhoon: mainly from the Arabic 'tufan', shaped by the Greek storm monster Typhon. The Cantonese 'tai fung', 'a great wind', may have shaped it too.

Kowtow: from the Chinese 叩頭 (k'o-t'ou), literally 'knock' + 'head', meaning to bow until your forehead touches the ground.

Four words, one language in their story. Which one surprised you?

## Facts

- Ketchup: “borrowed, directly or via Malay kecap (phonetically kətʃap) 'fish sauce,' from Southern Min (Chinese dialects of southeast Fujian) kôe-chiap (Xiamen), kê-chiap (Zhangzhou), from kôe, kê 'salted or pickled fish or shellfish' + chiap 'juice, sap'”. Its definition: ketchup is “usually made from tomatoes”. (source: Merriam-Webster, 'ketchup', https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/ketchup)
- Tycoon comes from Japanese 'taikun' “great lord”, a title of the shogun used with foreigners, from Chinese 大 “great” + 君 “lord” (Mandarin dà jūn). (source: Online Etymology Dictionary, 'tycoon', https://www.etymonline.com/word/tycoon (checked from search snippet))
- Typhoon comes mainly from Arabic 'tufan', shaped by Greek 'Typhon'; “Chinese (Cantonese) tai fung 'a great wind' also might have influenced the form or sense of the word in English”. So the video says Chinese may have shaped it, not that it is the origin. (source: Online Etymology Dictionary, 'typhoon', https://www.etymonline.com/word/typhoon (checked from search snippet))
- In Greek myth Typhon (Typhoeus) is a monster the storm winds come from. Quoted: "And from Typhoeus come boisterous winds which blow damply" (lines 869–870); he had "a hundred heads of a snake, a fearful dragon" (lines 824–825) and is called "the monster" (line 856). (source: Hesiod, Theogony, tr. H. G. Evelyn-White 1914, Perseus Digital Library, Tufts University, https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0130:card=853)
- Kowtow comes from Chinese k'o-t'ou 叩頭, “knock” + “head”: touching the forehead to the ground while kneeling. (source: Online Etymology Dictionary, 'kowtow', https://www.etymonline.com/word/kowtow (checked from search snippet))

Note: the etymonline pages block automated reading, so the tycoon, typhoon and kowtow entries were checked from search-result snippets of those pages.
