# Why is the sky blue, and why are sunsets red

## Idea

The sky is blue and sunsets are red for the same reason: air molecules scatter short blue wavelengths far more than long red ones (Rayleigh scattering), and at sunset light crosses so much air that the blue is scattered away before it reaches us.

## Hook

Same Sun, same air. So why is the sky blue at noon and red at sunset?

## Copy

Sunlight looks white, but it carries every colour, from short blue waves to long red ones.

When it hits the atmosphere, tiny nitrogen and oxygen molecules scatter it. Short wavelengths scatter far more: blue light about six times more than red. That scattered blue comes at you from every direction, so the whole sky glows blue.

At sunset the Sun is low, and its light crosses dozens of times more air to reach you. Along that long path most of the blue is scattered out sideways. What is left is orange and red.

Same air, different path: blue by day, red at dusk.

## Facts

- Sunlight contains all visible colours (wavelengths), from about 400 nm violet/blue to about 700 nm red. (source: NASA SciJinks, 'Why Is the Sky Blue?', https://scijinks.gov/blue-sky/)
- Air molecules scatter light with an intensity proportional to 1/wavelength⁴ (Rayleigh scattering), so shorter blue wavelengths scatter much more than red. (source: HyperPhysics (Georgia State University), 'Blue Sky', http://hyperphysics.phy-astr.gsu.edu/hbase/atmos/blusky.html)
- Blue light (≈450 nm) is scattered about 6 times more than red light (≈700 nm): (700/450)⁴ ≈ 5.9. (source: Arithmetic from the Rayleigh 1/λ⁴ law, HyperPhysics 'Blue Sky', http://hyperphysics.phy-astr.gsu.edu/hbase/atmos/blusky.html)
- The scattered blue light reaches us from all directions, which is why the whole sky looks blue. (source: NASA SciJinks, 'Why Is the Sky Blue?', https://scijinks.gov/blue-sky/)
- Sunlight at the horizon passes through about 38 times as much air as when the Sun is directly overhead. (source: Kasten & Young (1989), 'Revised optical air mass tables and approximation formula', Applied Optics 28(22); see Wikipedia 'Air mass (astronomy)')
- At sunset, the long path scatters most blue out of the beam, leaving mainly orange and red to reach the eye. (source: NOAA SciJinks, 'Why Are Sunsets Red?' / NASA SciJinks 'Why Is the Sky Blue?', https://scijinks.gov/blue-sky/)
